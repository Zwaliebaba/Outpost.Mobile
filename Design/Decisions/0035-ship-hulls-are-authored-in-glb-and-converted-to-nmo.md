# 0035 — Ship hulls are authored in GLB and converted to NMO, and ObjParser is deleted

Status: accepted
Date: 2026-08-30

## Context

[`Design/Archive/NmoFormat.md`](../Archive/NmoFormat.md) §13 assumed the shipping corpus would be the ten OBJ hulls
run through `Tools/ObjToNmo.py`, with `Exhaust` markers seeded once from the union-find clustering
that `ObjParser` had been running in the shipping loader on every boot. That is not what happened.
The hulls were re-authored in a modelling package and arrive as **GLB**, with their markers placed
by hand and carried as node `extras`; `Art/Meshes/GlbToNmo.py` converts them through the Blender
add-on. Eleven hulls exist where the OBJ corpus had ten, and they are authored per part rather than
per material — the Corvette is twelve submeshes where the OBJ was five, the Structure a hundred and
seventeen.

Two other things had to be decided as the swap landed. §14 ordered the marker consumers third and
the content swap fourth, with `MeshLibrary` "loading `.nmo` when present (OBJ fallback stays)". And
§14 slice 4 permitted keeping `ObjParser` for development import.

## Decision

**`Art/Meshes/*.glb` is the source of truth for hull geometry, materials and markers**, and
`Art/Meshes/GlbToNmo.py` is the converter. It sits beside the GLBs rather than in `Tools/`, where
AGENTS.md §2 puts content tools, because that is where an artist looks for it; the `Tools/` map row
points at it so the split is stated rather than discovered. `Tools/ObjToNmo.py` stays as the OBJ
path's record and as the Blender test's fixture source, not as the content pipeline.

**The content swap comes before the marker consumers**, so no OBJ fallback branch is ever written.

**`ObjParser` and `ObjParserTests` are deleted** with the twenty `.obj`/`.mtl` files, and
`ClusterAttachPoints` goes with them. `MeshData::attachPoints` stays for one more slice, filled by
`NmoReader` from the `Exhaust` markers, so `WorldView` does not change in the same commit the
format does.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the OBJ corpus as the shipping content and convert it with `ObjToNmo.py`, as §13 planned.**
  Rejected: the GLB hulls are the ones an artist is editing, and a pipeline whose input nobody
  authors is a pipeline that is already wrong. The heuristic's recovered exhausts are also strictly
  worse than the placed ones — anonymous points with no colour, no radius and no orientation.
- **Put `GlbToNmo.py` in `Tools/` with the rest of the content tools.** Rejected by the owner, and
  the reason is good: the converter reads the eleven files sitting next to it, and an artist who
  opens `Art/Meshes/` should find the thing that converts what is in it. The cost is one sentence
  in the map, which is cheaper than the misdirection.
- **Land the marker consumers first and give `MeshLibrary` an OBJ fallback**, as §14 ordered.
  Rejected: a branch written in one slice and deleted in the next is a branch nobody ever
  exercises, and it would have made the swap's screenshot ambiguous — a hull that looked wrong
  could have been the fallback rather than the reader.
- **Keep `ObjParser` for development import**, which §14 slice 4 permitted. Rejected: two mesh
  readers, one of which reads a format no asset in the tree uses, is easy to keep and hard to
  justify. This tree is greenfield and carries what it uses; the conventions the parser encoded —
  the Z negation, the `Kd` materials, the clustering — live on in `Tools/ObjToNmo.py`, which is
  where a heuristic that runs once belongs (0011).

## Consequences

- The corpus is regenerated by a command, not hand-edited. A hull that looks wrong is a defect in
  the GLB or in the converter, and the fix goes there; a hand-edited `.nmo` would be overwritten by
  the next conversion.
- The conversion is **structurally** reproducible, not byte-reproducible: the same GLBs through the
  same converter on a different Blender build differ in the last bits of near-zero normal and
  quaternion components, and in the payload CRC that follows them. Comparing a regenerated corpus
  means comparing the codec's read-back summary, not `fc /b`.
- `Fighter` and `Miner` ship without a `HULL_MESHES` row. They are hulls this corpus has and the
  game will want; putting one in the scene is a design question, not a format one.
- The engine no longer has a text mesh parser at all. Anything that wants to import an OBJ again
  converts it offline with `Tools/ObjToNmo.py` first.
