# 0016 — A planet or an asteroid is presentation, not a simulation entity

Status: accepted
Date: 2026-08-29

## Context

The first planet arrives in a game whose simulation is a plane of ships. `World` knows about hulls,
routes, orders, patrols and factions; the wire carries a ship record and nothing else. A planet is
several hundred metres of geometry with a shape, a position and a rotation, and it is the first
thing in the tree that is unmistakably *in the world* and yet has nothing to do with what the world
does.

The question is not "should it collide" — nothing collides with it today whichever way this goes.
The question is where it lives, because that is what decides whether adding one changes the wire
format, the replay contract and `GameLogicTests`.

## Decision

A body is presentation. It is generated by the composition root from a seed, uploaded once, drawn
by the client, and turned by `WorldView` on real time. `GameLogic` does not learn that bodies
exist: no entity, no table, no snapshot record, no tick that reads one. `Outpost/BodyCatalogue`
holds the six classes and their ramps; `WorldView::BodyView` holds the placement and the spin; a
ship flies straight through a rock.

## Alternatives considered

- **A `Body` entity in `GameLogic` with a snapshot record.** The honest shape if a body were ever
  going to matter to a tick, and it puts the position in one authoritative place instead of two.
  Rejected because nothing in the simulation reads it: it would add a wire record, a determinism
  surface and a despawn path for a value no rule consumes, and ADR 0009's own argument — a snapshot
  carries what a spectator needs, not what the simulation happens to hold — says a static decoration
  is not that. The cost of being wrong here is a slice; the cost of a wire field that turns out to
  be dead is every version of the protocol that carries it.
- **An immovable `HullSpec` row per asteroid.** The cheapest route to ships routing around rocks:
  the collision machinery of `Design/Archive/Collision.md` already handles a capsule that never moves, and
  `PlanRoute` would avoid one for free. Rejected *for now* rather than on principle — nothing in the
  game asks a ship to avoid a rock yet, and a `Structure` row per asteroid is a simulation change
  made for a screenshot. **This is the recorded route in**: when a body first has to be flown
  around, it becomes a hull row, and this record is what says so rather than a fresh argument.
- **Bodies in `WorldSimulation`, the executable's server-side adapter.** Rejected for ADR 0015's
  reason: authoritative state must not live in one of two executables. It does not arise anyway once
  the body is presentation.

## Consequences

- Two clients agree on a body only by agreeing on a seed. Today one client generates its own scene
  and there is nobody to disagree with; the day bodies matter to play, **the seed goes on the wire
  before the body does** — sixteen bytes for a world, which is the property slices 1 and 2 were
  written and pinned to keep.
- A ship ordered through an asteroid flies through it. That is visible in the first screenshot of
  this feature and is not a defect; it is this record.
- `GameLogicTests` is untouched by the whole feature, and the diff for slice 4 contains no file
  under `GameLogic/`. That is the evidence for this record, not a claim beside it.
- `WorldView` grows a second thing it owns and updates on real time, beside the explosion. The bound
  on that is the same as the explosion's: if a body ever needs to be read by something other than
  the renderer, this record is reopened rather than worked around.
